# 03 — Key Factors & Delivery Strategy

**Reads:** `outputs/01-site-brief.md`, `outputs/02-persona-journey.md`
**Writes:** `outputs/03-key-factors.md`

## Task
Find the overlap between **what the audience wants** and **what the company can actually deliver**, then decide the simplest, most convincing way to communicate it. Principle: **show, don't tell — and don't make visitors think hard.**

## Steps
1. List the audience's wants, needs, and objections (from step 02), ranked by importance to the buying decision.
2. List the company's real capabilities and proof (from the brief). Only use what's true and provable.
3. Match them. The overlap is the core message. Note wants the company can't meet (don't promise them) and strengths the audience doesn't care about (de-emphasize).
4. For each key factor, choose how to **show** it instead of claiming it.

## Output structure
- **Core message** — one sentence: who it's for + what they get + why this company. Plus 2–3 alternative phrasings.
- **Key factors table** — Audience want → Company proof → How to show it (photo, before/after, number, demo, video, testimonial, process diagram, guarantee, logo wall, live example) → Where on the site.
  - Bad: "We are trusted and professional." Good: "Logo row of 12 clients + '340 projects since 2015'."
- **Objection handling** — each objection → the answer → the proof → where it appears.
- **Cognitive load rules for this site** — e.g. one main CTA per page, max 3 options in any choice, plain words over jargon, scannable sections, visuals carry the message before text.
- **What to leave out** — features, claims, or content that would distract.
- **Missing proof** — evidence we need from the client to make the strongest case.
